Wishlist and acquisition

How the user says what they want and how MusicHub goes and gets it.

What is agreed

Accepted decisions in the logs.

The user curates the wishlist

  • The user decides what goes on the wishlist; MusicHub does not autonomously add artists, albums or tracks.
  • Once the user has added an item, MusicHub may search, download and upgrade within configured rules.

Source log: 2026-10-17-20-40-00-user-curated-wishlist.md

Search, recommendations and AI playlists

  • Users can search inside MusicHub and add chosen results to the wishlist.
  • MusicHub may show a Recommended list as suggestions without adding them automatically.
  • AI-generated playlists are expected to be a major source of suggestions, including music not yet in the library.
  • Discovery and recommendation are distinct from authorizing acquisition; wishlist additions remain user-controlled unless a later decision changes this.

Source log: 2026-10-17-20-45-00-music-search-recommendations-and-playlist-ai.md

Scope follows what was selected

  • Explicitly adding an album makes MusicHub resolve its track list and seek its tracks, subject to library and acquisition rules.
  • Adding a single track does not request its album; adding an artist does not request their discography.
  • When adding an album the user chooses the specific edition. The standard/original release is shown prominently at the top as the suggested default, other editions stay selectable, and no edition is silently substituted.

Source logs: 2026-10-17-20-50-00-explicit-album-wishlist-expansion.md, 2026-10-17-20-55-00-explicit-album-edition-selection.md

Album packages and per-track handling

  • MusicHub may acquire a whole album as a package even when some tracks are already present.
  • Each track is then evaluated separately against the library and quality profile: missing tracks are imported; existing tracks are upgraded only if the new file qualifies under the profile and cutoff, otherwise retained.
  • Downloaded contents are validated per track, not all-or-nothing: correctly identified tracks may be imported immediately even if others fail, failed tracks do not enter the library, and tracks still missing remain eligible for later searches.
  • Identity and the chosen edition are respected; a better-encoded but different mix, remaster or performance is not substituted.

Source logs: 2026-10-17-21-00-00-album-package-acquisition-and-per-track-upgrades.md, 2026-10-17-21-10-00-per-track-validated-album-import.md

Conceptual user journey

An illustrative sequence built from the decisions above; it names no screens, buttons or settings.

  1. The user searches in MusicHub, or looks at recommendations or an AI-generated playlist, and chooses what to add.
  2. For an album, the user picks an edition, with the standard/original release suggested first.
  3. MusicHub then searches for the wanted music within configured rules, possibly acquiring a whole album package.
  4. Each acquired track is validated individually; good ones are imported, bad ones are kept out, and gaps stay on the search list.
  5. Problems along the way appear on the problem dashboard (see Scheduler and problem dashboard).

What is not yet decided

  • Which search sources are supported and how results are grouped by track, album and artist.
  • How AI playlists reference unavailable music, whether one-click or bulk wishlist additions exist, and how recommendations are ranked or sourced.
  • What adding an artist means operationally, if it is supported at all; wishlist entry types and input methods.
  • How the authoritative original release is determined when several country/format editions exist; edition labeling; whether the default needs confirmation.
  • Handling of missing track listings or ambiguous album identity.
  • Whole-album versus individual-track preference; staging and cleanup of package downloads and rejected files; user review and reprocessing of rejected tracks; presentation of partial album completeness.
